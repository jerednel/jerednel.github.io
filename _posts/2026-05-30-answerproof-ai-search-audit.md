AI search visibility is not a rank tracker with a new label.

The useful question is narrower: when a buyer asks an AI system who they should
compare, does your company appear, do competitors appear, and which sources make
that answer likely?

That is the shape of Answerproof.

For a first pass, I start with one or more buyer questions that should plausibly
exist before a demo request:

```text
Which vendors should I compare for this problem?
What are the best tools or services in this category?
How does one named option compare with another?
What should a technical buyer know before choosing?
```

Then I check the answer surface for four things:

1. Whether the target is mentioned.
2. Whether competitors or adjacent firms are mentioned.
3. Which domains are cited as evidence.
4. Whether the cited sources explain the gap.

A small pre-check is not a full audit. It is a triage layer. If competitors show
up and the target does not, the next step is a focused diagnostic: more buyer
questions, more competitor domains, a source trail, and a 30-day implementation
plan.

That is the part most dashboards skip. A founder or growth lead does not only
need to know that visibility is low. They need to know what to change first:
comparison pages, entity coverage, schema, third-party citations, technical SEO,
partner listings, category explainers, or proof pages.

The wedge is also simple: prove the gap before asking anyone to buy a dashboard.
